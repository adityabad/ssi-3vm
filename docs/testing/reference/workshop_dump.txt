=== PAGE 9 ===
THE MISSING PIECE
Measuring Something Is Not Enough
“We will measure detection accuracy.” What accuracy is acceptable?
“We will measure response time.” What response time is acceptable?
“We will test the maximum load.” What load should the product handle?
Every test needs an ACCEPTANCE CRITERION.
Acceptance Criterion = The result that must be achieved for the test to PASS.

=== PAGE 22 ===
PILLAR 4 — USABILITY
Usability Testing
Can the intended user use it effectively?
Usability testing involves giving representative users realistic tasks and observing how effectively they can use the
product.
What can we observe or measure?
Task Number of Assistance Points of User
Time taken Errors made
completion attempts required confusion perception
Watch what representative users actually DO with your MVP — not what they say about it.
A usability test is only useful when the task is realistic and the user is
representative.
22

=== PAGE 23 ===
CHOOSING PARTICIPANTS
Who Should Be Your Usability Test Participant?
Choose people who:
✓ Belong to the intended user group
✓ Were not involved in developing the product
✓ Are reasonably unfamiliar with how the MVP works
Your teammates cannot be your usability test participants.
They already know:
• How the product works • What the intended workflow is
• What the controls mean • What the expected result should be
They are not fresh users.

Don’t Ask. Observe.
WEAK USABILITY TEST BETTER USABILITY TEST
